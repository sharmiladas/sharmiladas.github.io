Editorial practice

Editing an Article About AI Reliability

A worked edit with source checks and publication criteria

By Sharmila (Das) Rammohan
2026

The following practice draft contains unsupported claims about AI reliability. The revision narrows those claims, explains the remaining failure modes, and gives the reader a practical rollout recommendation. The review notes show why each change was made.

Practice draft

Today’s advanced AI models are rapidly becoming reliable reasoning engines that can solve almost any knowledge problem. Retrieval-augmented generation eliminates hallucinations because the model can look up facts instead of relying on memory. This means organizations can safely connect AI to internal documents and allow employees to use the answers for important decisions. The technology is already transforming every industry, and companies that do not deploy it now will be left behind.

The main remaining problem is adoption. Employees need training, and leaders need a governance committee. Once those pieces are in place, the system can produce trustworthy answers at scale. Citations also make the answers auditable because users can click the link and confirm that the model is correct. The goal should therefore be to ingest as much content as possible, connect the model to all available tools, and encourage teams to experiment freely.

Editorial assessment The draft overstates reliability, treats retrieval and citations as guarantees, and recommends expansion before the team has defined limits on use.

Revised article

An AI assistant connected to internal documents can help employees find and summarize information. Before relying on its answers, teams need to check which sources it uses and how it handles incomplete or conflicting evidence.

Retrieval-augmented generation gives a model access to external sources, which can improve specificity and make knowledge easier to update [1]. It does not eliminate unsupported answers. The system may retrieve an outdated source, miss a limiting condition, or produce a claim that goes beyond the supplied passage. A citation shows where the system looked; it does not by itself prove that the source supports the sentence.

Assign owners to the source collection and record which versions apply. Test whether retrieval finds enough evidence and whether the answer accurately reflects it. Questions involving unclear evidence or decisions with significant effects need a specialist review path.

Training employees is one part of rollout. Teams also need to test access controls, tool permissions, and the effect of changing source material. NIST’s generative-AI profile describes risk management across the lifecycle, including the work needed after deployment [2].

Begin with a specific use case and a source collection the team can maintain. Set limits on the assistant’s actions, test representative questions, and define what it should do when evidence is missing. Expand only after the workflow meets the criteria set for that use.

House-style excerpt

  • Define technical terms at first use; use the acronym only when it appears again.
  • Distinguish demonstrated capability from forecast, interpretation, and organizational aspiration.
  • Avoid anthropomorphic verbs such as understands or knows unless the article defines the intended technical meaning.
  • Attach citations to the smallest claim they support. Prefer primary research, standards, system reports, and first-party methodology.
  • State material limitations in the main narrative, not only in a footnote.
  • Use “safe” only for a defined context and evaluation; do not use it as an absolute property.
  • For claims that could affect important decisions, record the technical reviewer, evidence owner, approval decision, and publication date.

Final publication gate

  • Argument is coherent and proportionate to the evidence.
  • Every material factual claim has a verified source.
  • Technical reviewers have approved interpretation, not merely supplied comments.
  • Limitations and uncertainty are visible to the intended reader.
  • Title, summary, promotional copy, and social excerpts do not overstate the article.
  • CMS fields, links, metadata, accessibility, version, and approval record are complete.
