Adaptive learning prototype

Little Wins SAT

Free SAT quests for creative minds: art, history, language, and debate.

Try the free app →

The problem

Some teens know they need to prepare for the SAT but struggle to turn that intention into a regular study habit. They put off starting, lose track of what to practice, or stop when questions become frustrating. A long study plan gives them another task to manage, while repeated reminders from a parent can turn preparation into a source of tension.

These learners need a clear next step, a session that feels manageable, and feedback that helps them recover from mistakes. The product challenge is to make useful practice easier to start and easier to return to, while adapting the work to what the learner needs next.

Design question: How can an SAT tutor reduce the effort of getting started, choose useful practice, and support consistency without making a missed day feel like failure?

Who it is for

Teens who find self-directed SAT preparation difficult, especially learners who benefit from short tasks, a predictable routine, and immediate explanations. The design supports learner choice and does not assume that difficulty with consistency reflects a lack of ability.

A world built around curiosity

Explore an Art Studio, History Archive, Language Garden, or Debate Hall. Practice questions connect SAT skills to fictional exhibits, texts, artifacts, and arguments. Each question links to its concept lesson, worked example, and a Khan Academy course for deeper study.

Choose an explorer and one of six study-coach avatars, earn participation XP, and decorate a creative studio with pieces unlocked by completed sessions. All learning remains available at every level. There are no purchases, competitive leaderboards, speed bonuses, or penalties for missed days.

The product response

A small first step

Start a three- or five-question session directly from the home screen. A low-energy option makes it possible to practice without committing to a long session.

Practice that adjusts

Rules select questions using practice coverage, accuracy, and difficulty. Independent correct answers raise the level. Mistakes or hints lead to easier practice.

A reason to return

Missed questions become eligible for review the following day. Learners choose a weekly plan and can import recurring calendar reminders.

What you can try today

  • 92 original questions across eight math and reading/writing skill groups.
  • Hints, worked explanations, and a short strategy prompt after each answer.
  • A practice history, skill accuracy view, and weekly study-day goal.
  • Recurring learner calendar reminders and a separate daily parent/coach check-in file.
  • Short worked lessons linked from questions, an eight-question starting check, and a mistake notebook. Progress remains in the current browser.

Parent and coach support

A supporter can import a daily calendar check-in reminder at a time they choose. The learner can preview, edit, and copy a short progress note to share. The reminder is a static calendar alert, not an automatic report: the app does not email or text a parent or expose student activity to another device.

Prototype scope

This is an independent learning prototype with a small question bank. Adaptation uses explicit rules, and the explanations are authored content. It has no runtime AI chat, student accounts, cross-device sync, parent dashboard, SMS, or background push notifications. It does not simulate the full digital SAT or predict an SAT score.

Calendar alerts depend on the learner importing the reminder file and enabling alerts in their calendar. The app saves attempts on this device. Clearing browser data removes that history. The current backup can be downloaded but does not yet have an import feature.

What needs validation

The problem statement is a design hypothesis to test with teens and families. The prototype has not established that it improves SAT scores or study habits.

  • Can learners begin their first question without help?
  • How often do started sessions reach completion?
  • Do learners return after a missed day or a difficult question?
  • Does unaided accuracy improve on comparable questions, excluding hinted answers and simple recall?
  • Do learners find calendar reminders useful, and do they choose to keep them?

How it works

The app uses original HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. Explicit rules choose questions from practice coverage, unaided accuracy, difficulty, and review timing. The content and explanations are authored; no runtime AI model is required.

All app features are free. There is no subscription, student account, or payment gate.
