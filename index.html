Point of view

Reliable AI begins with reliable knowledge.

Teams often focus on the model or the prompt because those are the visible parts of an AI experience. Many failures begin earlier. The source may be outdated. Retrieval may select the wrong version. Ownership may be unclear. The quality test may reward fluent language while missing an inaccurate claim.

My work makes those decisions explicit. I help teams define which information can be used, who owns it, how it is retrieved, how quality is measured, and where human review belongs.

System view

Where I look when an AI answer goes wrong

A prompt is one part of the system. The problem may sit several steps earlier.

  1. Source

    Is the information current, approved, and owned?

  2. Retrieval

    Did the system find the right material and version?

  3. Context

    Did the useful evidence reach the model intact?

  4. Generation

    Did the output follow the task and constraints?

  5. Evaluation

    Did the tests catch factual and strategic errors?

  6. Review

    Did the right person make the final decision?
